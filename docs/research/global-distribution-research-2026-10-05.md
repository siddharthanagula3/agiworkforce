#### APAC-12

**Original claim:** JP-3: statute of July 2026 makes under 16 a hard rule; in force by Cabinet Order no later than 2028-07-16

**Original verdict:** confirmed, with a correction to the reading. **Lane/group:** East Asia.

**Supported meaning and limits recorded by the verifier:** e-Gov shows amending Act No. 56 of 2026, promulgated 2026-07-17, this revision "UnEnforced", scheduled 2028-07-16, "within two years by Cabinet Order". New Art. 40-2 swaps "the person" for "the person's legal representative" in the listed consent and notice articles (18, 20(2), 21, 27, 28, 31 and others). Three exceptions, not one: justified ignorance of age, a minor licensed to trade, no legal representative. The lane's "needs the legal representative's consent" overstates: the APPI does not require consent for ordinary collection and use, so the rule bites where consent or notice is already required (use beyond purpose, sensitive data, third-party and cross-border provision).

- <a id="usam-n124"></a> **USAM-N124** (`v-us-americas.md:124`), original verifier commentary: - United States, federal: nothing in force requires parental consent or age verification for 13 to 17. COPPA stops at 12. Four bills that would change this are pending and none is law.

- <a id="usam-n125"></a> **USAM-N125** (`v-us-americas.md:125`), original verifier commentary: - United States, states: no statute read requires verified parental consent for a 13 to 17 year old to use a general web assistant. What the statutes do is attach duties the moment the service knows a user is a minor, and a stored age is knowledge (NY AG). The duties with no size threshold: New York (separate informed consent for anything not strictly necessary, research and development expressly excluded, no sale); Connecticut (outright bans, no consent route, since 2026-07-01); Colorado and Montana (no sale, targeted ads or off-purpose processing without the minor's consent); Arkansas (13 to 16, consent can be in the terms).

- <a id="usam-n126"></a> **USAM-N126** (`v-us-americas.md:126`), original verifier commentary: - App-store laws put verified parental consent into the store, not the developer's sign-up: Texas now for mobile apps, Alabama from 2027-01-01, Utah from 2027-05-06, Louisiana from 2027-07-01. In those states terms cannot be enforced against a minor without store-verified consent. California's Digital Age Assurance Act (2027-01-01) is different: it passes an age-bracket signal, with no parental-consent step, and the signal counts as actual knowledge on the website too.

- <a id="usam-n127"></a> **USAM-N127** (`v-us-americas.md:127`), original verifier commentary: - California SB 1119 applies only if the product is a "companion chatbot". If it is, self-declared age is not one of the named routes, and parent-only default settings apply to child users from 2027-07-01.

- <a id="usam-n128"></a> **USAM-N128** (`v-us-americas.md:128`), original verifier commentary: - Quebec: collecting from a child under 14 needs parental consent (s. 4.1). Chile from 2026-12-01: under 14 needs parental consent. Colombia: any minor's data needs the legal representative's authorisation. Brazil: parental consent under 12; under 16 cannot contract; the statute's art. 24 on its words requires accounts of under-16s to be linked to a guardian's account; the regulator's draft guide says a self-declared age does not take a generative AI service out of scope.

- <a id="usam-n129"></a> **USAM-N129** (`v-us-americas.md:129`), original verifier commentary: - Canada federally: 13 to 17 may consent if the process is adapted to their maturity; under 13 needs a parent.

- <a id="usam-n131"></a> **USAM-N131** (`v-us-americas.md:131`), original verifier commentary: My reading. A flat "13 and over with a parent's permission" is not unlawful in the United States today, but it creates knowledge, and with knowledge the Free plan's training-capable providers become the problem in New York and Connecticut. Outside the United States the floor of 13 is too low in Quebec, Chile from December 2026, Brazil and Colombia, and a clause asserting permission is not the parental consent those laws describe. OpenAI's own wording avoids this by saying "13 or the minimum age required in your country". Uncertain: whether the product is a companion chatbot under the California, Washington and Oregon definitions, and how Brazil's art. 24 will be applied outside social networks.

#### Q2. Is an adults-only service with a stored self-declared "18 or older" confirmation defensible, and what is said about self-declaration?

- <a id="usam-n135"></a> **USAM-N135** (`v-us-americas.md:135`), original verifier commentary: What the sources say.

- <a id="usam-n136"></a> **USAM-N136** (`v-us-americas.md:136`), original verifier commentary: - FTC: general-audience operators need not investigate age; the Commission declined in 2025 to require more than self-declared age; storing age information does not breach the Rule. It also wrote in February 2026 that self-declaration raises "concerns about the effectiveness", and its FAQ says a checkbox is not a neutral screen and that asking for age and then not acting on it creates liability.

- <a id="usam-n137"></a> **USAM-N137** (`v-us-americas.md:137`), original verifier commentary: - States: Colorado, Arkansas, Maryland and Nebraska say expressly that they do not require age collection or age-gating. None of the statutes read treats an adult self-declaration as a violation. A recorded "under 18" answer is actual knowledge.

- <a id="usam-n138"></a> **USAM-N138** (`v-us-americas.md:138`), original verifier commentary: - Brazil: the regulator's draft says terms, notices and simple self-declaration do not remove likely access, and the statute forbids self-declaration where content is prohibited for minors.

- <a id="usam-n139"></a> **USAM-N139** (`v-us-americas.md:139`), original verifier commentary: - Contract: a stored declaration of majority bars disaffirmance by statute in at least Kansas and Washington and under Brazil's Civil Code art. 180 for ages 16 and 17.

- <a id="usam-n140"></a> **USAM-N140** (`v-us-americas.md:140`), original verifier commentary: - Pending: the GUARD Act would reject a checkbox or birth-date entry, but only for "AI companions".

- <a id="usam-n142"></a> **USAM-N142** (`v-us-americas.md:142`), original verifier commentary: My reading. Flow B is defensible in the United States and in Canada, Mexico, Chile, Argentina and Colombia on what I read: no rule found demands more from an adults-only general assistant, and the record has evidential value. It is weakest in Brazil, where the regulator has said in draft that it will look past it, with enforcement from January 2027. Two design points follow from the sources: the question should not be a pre-worded checkbox if it is meant to screen (FTC FAQ), and an answer of "no" must lead somewhere (block and delete), because that answer is knowledge. "18" is not adult in Nebraska or British Columbia; "18 or the age of majority where you live, whichever is higher" covers that. Uncertain: whether California's SB 1119 applies at all.

#### Q3 to Q7, where this group bears on them

- <a id="usam-n146"></a> **USAM-N146** (`v-us-americas.md:146`), original verifier commentary: Q3 (provider and platform terms). The four lanes do not cover API terms. From this group: Anthropic's consumer terms are 18+, OpenAI's are 13+ with permission; these govern their own apps. App stores become a conduit of legal duties by statute in Texas, Utah, Louisiana, Alabama and California.

- <a id="usam-n148"></a> **USAM-N148** (`v-us-americas.md:148`), original verifier commentary: Q4 (acceptance and marketing consent). Ninth Circuit cases require a conspicuous sentence that names the action ("By clicking Continue, you agree to the Terms") and a button with the same word; an "I agree" click or checkbox is presumptively enforceable. Claude's screen today does not mention the Terms and ChatGPT's has only footer links, so copying either leaves acceptance resting on use alone. Marketing: in the United States no prior consent is needed (CAN-SPAM), so a bundled sentence is lawful there. For Canadian recipients consent must be "sought separately" and the sender must prove it, so a bundled sentence does not work.

- <a id="usam-n150"></a> **USAM-N150** (`v-us-americas.md:150`), original verifier commentary: Q5 (training disclosure). Canadian regulators: training on conversations is not integral to the service, a one-time notice at sign-up was not enough, express consent was required where content is sensitive or the use unexpected, and a choice must be offered. New York: for a known 13 to 17 year old, research and development needs separate informed consent with refusal as the most prominent option, and passing data for consideration to anyone who is not a processor is a prohibited sale. Connecticut: prohibited for known minors. COPPA: for a known under-13, disclosure for AI training needs separate verifiable parental consent. FTC staff: moving to AI training by a quiet change of terms may be unfair or deceptive. My reading: tell people before the first prompt that Free-plan content goes to providers who may train on it, and give a real way to avoid it; a route with no-training terms for anyone known to be a minor.

- <a id="usam-n152"></a> **USAM-N152** (`v-us-americas.md:152`), original verifier commentary: Q6 (duties from asking and storing an age). A stated age under 13 is actual knowledge under COPPA: delete or obtain verifiable parental consent. A stated age under 18 is actual knowledge in New York (30-day delete-or-consent clock), Colorado, Montana, Connecticut and Arkansas. Under California's DAAA from 2027 a developer must prefer its own "clear and convincing" age information over a conflicting store signal. Brazil's art. 13 limits data collected for age verification to that purpose. The FTC accepts that an operator need not retain the exact age.

- <a id="usam-n154"></a> **USAM-N154** (`v-us-americas.md:154`), original verifier commentary: Q7 (next 18 months). 2026-11: ANPD sanction regulations due. 2026-12-01: Chile. 2026-12-31: Colorado cure period ends. 2027-01-01: California DAAA, AB 2246, SB 1119 section 21811 and the 22602 amendment; Washington HB 2225; Oregon SB 1546; Vermont Act 63; Alabama HB 161. 2027-01: ANPD active enforcement. 2027-05-06: Utah. 2027-07-01: SB 1119 sections 21812 and 21813; Louisiana; DAAA backfill. 2028-01-01: SB 1119 interface testing. No date: COPPA 2.0, KIDS Act, KOSA, GUARD Act; the FTC's promised COPPA review on age verification; New York CDPA rules; the OPC children's code; the Fifth Circuit's merits ruling on Texas SB 2420.
