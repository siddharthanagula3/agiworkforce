Founder clarification, October 12: no paying customers; paid access was intentionally gated to learn demand; monthly advertising budget is $0. The waitlist is a deliberate experiment, not evidence that buyers rejected an available paid offer.

**Run a $0 organic demand experiment while preserving the intentional paid waitlist. ## 5. What they would pay for

Customers buy an accepted output and a dependable process. Potential value includes fewer setup/revision steps, reusable client criteria, easier evidence checking and lower formatting effort. Each needs direct measurement.

For the first task, compare:

`approved brief + same source packet + acceptance rubric → options comparison → recommendation → genuine update/revision → required editable output`

Measure active time from setup through acceptance, waiting time separately, all founder help, unsupported material claims, recovery, cost and export repairs. Preserve first drafts so later correction cannot erase earlier failure. The incumbent is the participant's own tool and method. If source collection is supplied, the experiment measures supplied-source synthesis; it does not prove open-web research superiority.

A savings hypothesis is only financially meaningful when the buyer can use the released time. Thirty minutes at a nominal billing rate is not automatically thirty minutes of new revenue. Ask whether it reduces uncompensated revision, releases capacity for sold work or improves a real deadline.

Use the current eligible offer first. Pro's inspected $20/month is a learning anchor, not a validated optimal price. Demonstrated demand might eventually support more valuable packaging, but this report does not reprice the product or imply that $50/$100 would be accepted. If the founder provides manual research or customization, disclose it, record the labor and separate any service revenue from unassisted software demand.

Willingness-to-pay evidence, strongest first: a reconciled real payment plus renewal and use; a real payment after an accepted task; continuation at a specific attainable offer; a price conversation; a signup/waitlist entry. Compliments and hypothetical survey prices do not count as transactions.

## 7. Positioning, switching and defensibility

**Proposed initial category:** a workspace for recurring client research briefs. The broader ecosystem remains the product ambition; the first sales invitation should make a single attainable task clear.

Proposed headline: “Turn a client brief into a draft you can review.” Support: “Bring approved references and decision criteria. Compare options, check the draft and revise it in AGI Web.” CTA: “Try it with a public brief.” These are founder-authored drafts, not customer quotations or measured promises.

The purchase has to answer: why add or replace a tool, why now, what survives migration, what still requires review, where data goes, what the full bill is, and how to leave with usable work. Account familiarity, saved projects, employer approval and existing bundles create switching friction.

| Proposed benefit            | Evidence needed                                                           | What would defeat it                                      |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Less context reconstruction | Lower setup/revision time on the second distinct task                     | Existing project does this just as well                   |
| Easier checking             | All material claims supported; lower checking effort                      | Attractive citations hide unsupported statements          |
| Usable client output        | Required export opens with acceptable tables, links and editing           | Repair takes longer than the incumbent                    |
| Provider choice/control     | Buyer changes route for a real requirement and prefers the resulting work | Choice adds decisions without improving the outcome       |
| Affordable delivery         | Reconciled total cost per accepted task within the paid offer             | Retries, tools, support or heavy usage erase contribution |

A possible durable advantage is the accumulated fit between a specific workflow, tested outputs, practitioner distribution and reusable customer-controlled context. Those assets have not been established yet. Foundation-model access and UI parity are readily available inputs. Keep customer data portable and deletion trustworthy; lock-in is not a substitute for preference.

Useful customer language from published stories includes “Time is our biggest asset and also our biggest enemy” from Pragmatic and “The hard part is not giving people access to AI” from Percepture. These are separate vendor-selected organizations, not AGI interview findings or a representative sample. They suggest interview topics about time, shared context and adoption. ## 9. Economics to know before buying a click

Use observed cohort cash and variable cost. A consumer plan does not fund API usage automatically; AGI must pay its own serving route. A provider quota, promotion or low nominal price does not guarantee ongoing cost or capacity. Keep contract/price/routing owners canonical and reverify terms before release or spending.

Define:

- **Monthly contribution per paying account:** subscription and top-up revenue earned, net of refunds/discounts, less provider/tools/compute/storage/egress, processor/FX fees, support labor and an explicit abuse/dispute allowance. Exclude tax collected for remittance from revenue.
- **CAC:** attributable acquisition spend, creative/tool costs and founder sales/onboarding effort divided by new, verified external paying accounts. Report cash CAC and labor-inclusive CAC separately. At zero verified payers, CAC is undefined.
- **Contribution payback:** time until cumulative observed cohort contribution recovers CAC. Include the free-trial/waitlist interval before cash arrives and customers that never renew.
- **Maximum affordable CAC:** expected contribution within the founder's chosen cash-recovery window, less a safety reserve. If contribution is negative, paid acquisition magnifies the loss.
- **Break-even customers:** fixed monthly operating costs plus required founder draw, divided by positive monthly contribution per account. Fixed costs and personal runway were not provided.

The model below is illustrative arithmetic, **not measured AGI margins or recommended bids**. Pro uses the inspected $20 monthly catalogue price. Each example assumes an external payer remains for three months and total variable costs, including support, are $6/month.

| Hypothetical quantity                                    | Calculation   | Result |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------- | ------ |
| Monthly contribution                                     | $20 − $6      | $14    |
| Three-month contribution before acquisition              | 3 × $14       | $42    |
| Affordable CAC after a 20% illustrative reserve          | $42 × 0.8     | $33.60 |
| CPC ceiling at an assumed 2% click-to-external-paid rate | $33.60 × 0.02 | $0.672 |
| CPC ceiling at an assumed 5% rate                        | $33.60 × 0.05 | $1.68  |

If a $2 click converts to a payer at 2%, media CAC is $100 before labor and creative. This exceeds the scenario's $42 contribution. At 5%, media CAC is $40, still above its reserve-adjusted ceiling. Every conversion, survival and cost assumption must be replaced with observations before using these figures for a campaign. With monthly churn probability `q`, a scenario contribution over `H` months is `Σ contribution_t × (1−q)^(t−1)`; constant churn is itself an assumption. No reliable AGI `q` was measured.

At the catalogue price, 100 Pro accounts would mean $2,000 gross monthly subscription revenue and 1,000 would mean $20,000, before any costs. Under the illustrative $14 contribution, a $5,000 combined fixed-cost/founder-draw target would require 358 retained accounts. These are planning scenarios, not forecasts, a serviceable-market estimate or evidence such customers exist.

Stress-test the actual paid cohort: ordinary and heavy users; long context and caching; research/tool retries; failed tasks; image/video; sandbox/browser time; payment fees and refunds; support minutes; abuse; and provider withdrawal/price change. Measure p50 and p95 costs and accepted outcomes by workflow/plan. A low average can hide a loss-making heavy tail. Existing ledger controls help, but the inspected estimated/test-contaminated records do not demonstrate profitability.

## 11. How to prove or reject the first hypothesis

Use the existing [validation kit](../../audit/plan/first-customer-validation.md), including its artifact-backed discovery script, five paired real tasks, assistance ledger and two-week repeat observation. Preserve negative results and satisfied incumbent users. Proposed gates guide scarce founder effort; they are not statistically demonstrated PMF thresholds.

The first month should resolve whether a recurring job exists, whether AGI improves the attainable workflow, whether the prospect returns without rescue, and whether they take a concrete next step after seeing a specified price and limits. Keep the paid gate: a price-qualified waitlist entry, approved upcoming task or voluntary repeat is useful demand evidence, but not a transaction. Actual payment testing becomes a later step only if the founder explicitly opens limited access. A closed checkout is not a customer refusal. Incentivized use and personal relationships must be labelled separately.

The open plan owns the current $0 demand experiment and the deferred commercial gates. First independently classify candidate accounts, verify consent-compatible measurement and observe real-task preference, voluntary repeat and price-qualified waitlist behavior. If paid access is later opened, reconcile payments/costs and observe renewal. Any future advertising requires a new nonzero budget decision and evidence from a paid cohort; the calendar may extend beyond 30 days for billing-cycle observations.

Reject or revise the job if the incumbent remains preferred, review/export repairs erase the saving, the task rarely recurs, permission prevents use or the economics fail. Build one demonstrated missing step in its existing owner, then retest fresh work. Successful founder-assisted delivery can validate a service offer; it cannot silently establish self-serve software fit.
