# Audit Completeness — a self-assessment of the 2026-08-15 parity audit

**Assessed:** 2026-08-15 · **Commit:** `e15df56e3` (`compliance/dpdp`), working tree clean
**Scope of this document:** the 12 headline deliverables in `audit/parity-2026-08-15/` (`BenchmarkResearch.md`, `CurrentProductInventory.md`, `ScreenInventory.md`, `CapabilityMatrix.md`, `SurfaceParityMatrix.md`, `FrontendGapAudit.md`, `BackendGapAudit.md`, `GapMatrix.md`, `DeadAndDisconnectedCode.md`, `MissingScreensAndComponents.md`, `CrossPlatformArchitectureAudit.md`, `PriorityExecutionPlan.md`), cross-checked against their underlying `research/`, `inventory/`, and `gaps/` evidence files.

**Method.** Every deliverable was read in full or near-full (structure via section headers, content via targeted passes); every claim examined below was checked against its cited source file, not accepted from the synthesis prose alone. Where this document says a claim is stale, contradicted, or unsupported, the contradicting/correcting text is quoted or line-cited from the audit's own files — this is not new investigation, it is cross-referencing work the synthesis layer should have done and in a few identified places did not.

This is not a re-audit. It does not re-derive severities, re-run greps, or challenge the 168 filed gaps on their merits. It asks the two questions the task set: **what did this audit not look at, and where does it disagree with itself?**

## 1. What this audit round covers well — briefly, for calibration

Before the gaps: this is a genuinely deep pass, and the critique below should be read against that baseline, not instead of it.

- **Evidence discipline is real, not decorative.** A spot-check of ~40 claims across all 12 documents during this review found citations that resolved to what they claimed in every case checked; several claims explicitly carry `NEEDS_VALIDATION`/`UNVERIFIED` labels rather than being asserted as fact (e.g. `web-backend.md:699` enterprise SSO/SCIM, `desktop-tauri.md:50-66` the four allowlist-bypassing renderer modules).
- **Two retractions from earlier drafts are handled correctly and consistently.** The "duplicate sign-in routes" and "product not publicly reachable" findings were wrong, and every document that touches those areas (`CurrentProductInventory.md:31-42`, `CapabilityMatrix.md:29-38`, `ScreenInventory.md:59`, `BenchmarkResearch.md:468`, `BackendGapAudit.md:845-864`, `PriorityExecutionPlan.md:723-731`, `web-route-sweep-findings.md:38-63`, `inventory/deployment-state.md:125`) states the corrected position rather than the original error. This is a genuinely disciplined result — see §5 for the one place it needed checking most carefully.
- **Adversarial re-verification of prior "Done" claims is real, not nominal.** `gaps/done-claim-verification.md` re-checked 71 rows, sent every non-`CONFIRMED_DONE` verdict to an independent skeptic agent to try to refute it, and 0 of 9 challenges were refuted. That is a meaningful correctness check most audits skip.
- **The audit is honest about its own scope pressure.** `prior-art-reconciliation.md` states plainly which categories the prior 341-row tracker structurally could not see (backend/runtime, cross-surface duplication, dead code, end-to-end tracing) and sequences this round's effort accordingly. `MissingScreensAndComponents.md` §A.7 explicitly labels the CLI's absence from its own screen-gap table "a genuine coverage gap in the audit itself, recorded honestly rather than papered over" — self-disclosure that most of the gaps below build on rather than discover from scratch.

The critique below is therefore mostly about **propagation**, not detection: in several cases the audit's own later, more rigorous passes (a domain gap file, a `known-flaws.md` cross-check) found something the earlier synthesis got wrong, and the correction did not make it back into the headline document a reader is most likely to skim.

## 3. Thin-or-no-coverage areas

### 3.1 The CLI surface — thin, and the audit says so, but the thinness is worse than the one self-disclosure states

`MissingScreensAndComponents.md` §A.7 is honest about one dimension of this ("zero rows in `ui-gaps.csv`... a genuine coverage gap in the audit itself"). Checking the rest of the deliverables shows the gap is broader than that one paragraph implies:

- **No dedicated inventory file.** Every other surface has one (`web-frontend.md`, `web-backend.md`, `mobile.md`, `desktop-tauri.md`, `desktop-electron.md`, `extension-chrome.md`, `extension-vscode.md`). The CLI's only inventory source is `inventory/runtime-infra.md` §4 (lines 183–267, ~85 lines, titled "the `developer engine`") plus a direct read of `apps/cli/src/lib.rs:644-795`'s `enum Command`. That section is competent as far as it goes (it correctly flags the honest `mcp-server` stub, the Windows sandbox gap, the Linux `bwrap`-vs-`seccomp` split) but it inventories _existence_, not _UX_ — there is no walkthrough of what any of the 22 top-level commands or the 13 TUI overlay screens (`ScreenInventory.md` §1.7: agent picker, approval overlay, cost HUD, diff review, effort picker, model picker, skills toggle, statusline setup, theme picker, etc.) actually look or feel like next to Codex CLI's or Claude Code CLI's equivalent.
- **Zero screenshot evidence of the competitor's CLI/TUI.** The research corpus's 9 `shots-*.md` files (288 screenshots) cover macOS, iOS, web, and the VS Code extension for both competitors — and the _desktop app's embedded terminal panel_ (`shots-codex-macos-shell.md` §2.1) — but none cover the standalone `codex` or `claude` CLI's own terminal UI. Everything this audit knows about competitor CLI behavior comes from prose research (`chatgpt-work-codex.md` §4-§7, `claude-code-chrome-ide.md` §2) sourced from docs, GitHub, and press — real and well-cited, but a different evidentiary tier from the control-by-control screenshot comparisons every other surface got.
- **`GapMatrix.md`'s own totals confirm the imbalance is structural, not incidental.** CLI carries 3 of 168 gaps (0 P0 / 1 P1 / 2 P2 / 0 P3) — the joint-thinnest surface alongside VS Code (2 gaps) and Electron (3 gaps), against Web's 45 and Desktop-Tauri's 31. Unlike VS Code (which has a 471-line dedicated inventory, a 862-test CI suite, and a full `commandParity.test.ts` cross-check) and Electron (deliberately thin by founder design, per `apps/desktop/AGENTS.md`), the CLI's low count is not backed by an equivalent depth of static or dynamic verification — it is low because the audit spent less time there, not because there was less to find. `apps/cli` is 743+ Rust files' worth of surface area sharing crates with Desktop; a CLI-equivalent domain pass (a `domain-cli.md`, or at minimum a dedicated `inventory/cli.md`) was never commissioned this round.
- **`CapabilityMatrix.md` has no CLI-specific section.** Its 23 numbered sections are organized by capability, and CLI is mentioned only incidentally (3 hits in 297 lines) inside rows about shared crates or sandboxing — there is no "CLI development-agent parity" row comparable to the depth given to Chat, Composer, Artifacts, or Agentic Work.
- **`PriorityExecutionPlan.md`'s Phase 8 (CLI)** cites exactly two gap IDs (`BACKEND-RUNTIME-009`, `BACKEND-RUNTIME-010`), both about sandboxing on Windows/Linux — real findings, but neither touches the CLI's actual interactive experience, slash-command surface, or its TUI relative to Codex's/Claude Code's.

**Next investigation this would require:** a dedicated CLI domain pass structured like the other seven surfaces — a `inventory/cli.md` walking every one of the 22 top-level commands and 13 TUI screens against `apps/cli/src/tui/*.rs` source, cross-referenced against `chatgpt-work-codex.md`/`claude-code-chrome-ide.md`'s prose descriptions of Codex CLI/Claude Code CLI control-by-control (approval-mode UX, plan-mode rendering, model/effort picker shape, checkpoint/rewind UX, subagent/worktree surfacing) — the same rigor `ScreenInventory.md` Part 2 already gives every other surface via screenshots. If actual competitor CLI screenshots or session recordings can be obtained, that would close the evidentiary-tier gap noted above.

### 3.2 Dynamic web routes — excluded by construction, covered unevenly

`local-route-sweep.tsv` covers only the 146 static routes; `[param]` segments are excluded by construction. `ScreenInventory.md` §1.1.8 states this plainly: dynamic routes "are covered only where `web-frontend.md` read their source directly." That is true, but the coverage that exists is inconsistent in depth:

| Route                             | What was checked                                                                                                       | What was not                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `/chat/[sessionId]`               | Source read (`WebChatPage.tsx`), classified `COMPLETE`                                                                 | Never requested with a nonexistent/foreign-owned session ID; no confirmation of the actual error/redirect behavior |
| `/share/[token]`                  | Source read, `use-share-conversation.ts:98` cited                                                                      | Never requested with an expired or already-revoked token                                                           |
| `/plugins/[id]`, `/skills/[name]` | Classified `COMPLETE` in `CurrentProductInventory.md` §2.4                                                             | No evidence of a not-found-slug or malformed-id path being checked                                                 |
| `/connect/[deviceType]`           | Listed as one of "7 distinct multi-step screens," `COMPLETE`                                                           | No enumeration of what `deviceType` values are valid or what an invalid one does                                   |
| `/pair/[code]`                    | Explicitly and correctly flagged: "`[code]` deliberately not read server-side... nothing mints a code-bearing URL yet" | This is the one dynamic route given real scrutiny — a useful positive contrast showing what the others lack        |

None of this changes any current classification — nothing here contradicts a `COMPLETE` verdict — but every one of these routes' error/edge-case behavior is currently **asserted by absence of contrary evidence**, not verified. A malformed `[sessionId]`, an expired `[token]`, or an invalid `[deviceType]` value are exactly the inputs most likely to produce an unhandled exception or a confusing blank state, and none was exercised.

**Next investigation:** an HTTP sweep of representative dynamic-route instances (a real conversation ID, a deliberately invalid one, an expired share token, a `/skills/does-not-exist` slug) against `next dev`, the same method already proven for the 146 static routes — this is a same-day extension of existing tooling, not new infrastructure.

### 3.3 Authenticated / running-app behavior — the largest coverage gap in the audit, and it is under-flagged relative to its size

This is the single biggest finding of this completeness review. The audit's own `inventory/web-route-sweep-findings.md` states the limitation about as plainly as possible, in its closing section:

> "**What this sweep does not establish** — Nothing about authenticated behaviour. 33 routes were only observed redirecting; their contents were not exercised. ... Nothing about client-side runtime errors, network failures, or interaction behaviour — a 200 with a rendered shell says nothing about whether the controls inside it work."

That caveat is scoped narrowly (to the 146-route web sweep) and appears in one inventory file. It does not propagate into the vocabulary used by the twelve headline deliverables. Across `CurrentProductInventory.md`, `ScreenInventory.md`, `CapabilityMatrix.md`, and `SurfaceParityMatrix.md`, dozens of `COMPLETE` verdicts for authenticated-only surfaces — chat, Settings (all ~19 sections), Billing/Stripe checkout, the Admin panel, Projects, Library, Schedules — are backed entirely by **reading source** (does the component import the right hook, does the API route exist and look wired, does a test assert the call) and **not** by anyone or anything observing the rendered, signed-in product. Concretely:

- **No signed-in web session was ever driven**, by a human or by tooling, at any point in this audit round. The only accessibility CI gate that touches the real app (`DESIGN-SYSTEM-003`, `GapMatrix.md` ~line 2125) audits exactly one authenticated destination (in this case: zero — its 5 pages are unauthenticated) and Desktop's equivalent gate stays signed-out "to keep the audit deterministic," per its own code comment cited in that finding. `GapMatrix.md`'s own recommendation for that gap — "add a signed-in fixture (Clerk test user / mocked session)... open the Settings modal, a populated chat thread, and one or two dialogs" — is exactly the missing step for this audit's own verification method, not only for the product's a11y CI, but the finding is filed and framed as a _product_ CI gap, not restated anywhere as an _audit-methodology_ gap.
- **Desktop-Tauri's 1,268-command surface** has one real, CI-enforced piece of dynamic evidence (the registration-drift test proving every frontend call resolves to a real command) — but nothing in this audit confirms any of the ~1,114 "called from live UI" commands actually behaves correctly when invoked; that inference rests on the command being called from a component that itself was only read, not run.
- **Billing is the sharpest example of the risk.** `CurrentProductInventory.md:117` states "real Stripe checkout, real Customer Portal, overage/headroom toggle matches recent commit history" as `COMPLETE` — accurate as a description of what the code does, but no checkout flow was ever run end-to-end (test-mode or otherwise) in this audit; the claim is "the code that implements Stripe checkout is well-formed," not "a checkout was observed succeeding."
- **The one place this gets a partial fix is `DESIGN-SYSTEM-003`**, and it is scoped to accessibility only. There is no equivalent finding, anywhere in the 168 filed gaps, for functional correctness — i.e., no gap exists that says "this audit's `COMPLETE` classifications for authenticated surfaces are source-verified but runtime-unverified, and that should be closed before treating them as ship-ready."

To be clear about what this is **not** saying: it is not evidence that anything is broken. The source-reading discipline elsewhere in this audit (tracing `UI → state → API/IPC → runtime → persistence → UI`, per its own stated method) is a legitimate and unusually rigorous way to establish wiring. But "wired in source" and "confirmed to render and work for a signed-in user" are different confidence tiers, and the audit's own vocabulary (`COMPLETE`) does not distinguish them at the point of assertion. A reader of `CurrentProductInventory.md` alone would have no way to know that essentially none of its `COMPLETE` verdicts for product surfaces rest on anyone having actually seen the authenticated product render.

**Next investigation:** the fix `DESIGN-SYSTEM-003` already prescribes for the product's own CI, reused as an audit method — a seeded/mocked Clerk session (or a real test account) driven once through Playwright/MCP browser tooling across: `/chat` with a populated thread, the Settings modal (all ~19 sections), one Stripe checkout attempt in test mode, `/admin`, and one dynamic route from §3.2 in each state (valid and invalid param). This does not need to be exhaustive — even one authenticated pass per major surface would upgrade dozens of `COMPLETE` verdicts from "source-verified" to "observed," and would very likely surface the kind of interaction/runtime-error class of bug the sweep's own closing note predicts is invisible to a source read.

### 3.4 Per-response-type state coverage — self-disclosed, correctly, but not tracked forward

`FrontendGapAudit.md` §3.2 is a model of honest scoping: it lists five response-part types with confirmed, cited state machines (tool call, deep research, code execution, image, interactive/schema cards) and then states directly:

> "**Explicitly NOT assessed in this audit round, per the evidence-or-silence standard** — no component-level state machine for these was located or verified, and none is asserted here: **timeout**, **rate-limited**, **permission-denied** (distinct from tool-approval), and a per-part-type **empty-state** taxonomy... flagged as **NEEDS VALIDATION** for the next round rather than guessed at."

This is exactly the right way to handle an unassessed area. The gap is downstream: this disclosure has no corresponding `GapMatrix.md` ID, is not listed in `PriorityExecutionPlan.md`'s verification steps for any phase, and is not cross-referenced from `CapabilityMatrix.md` §3 (Message rendering) or `SurfaceParityMatrix.md` §2.1 — both of which present response-part coverage tables without a footnote pointing back to this caveat. A reader who only sees the summary tables in those two documents has no way to discover that timeout/rate-limit/permission-denied states were never checked at all, on any surface.

**Next investigation:** grep each surface's tool-call/response renderer for `timeout`, `rate_limit`, `429`, and a permission-denied-vs-approval-required distinction; if no state machine is found (the likely outcome given the sibling states above required deliberate, named implementation), file it as a proper `RENDERING-0xx` gap with a severity call, rather than leaving it as a footnote in one of twelve documents.

### 3.6 Production deployment identity — a systemic caveat under-stated relative to its reach

`CurrentProductInventory.md` §1 is honest about this narrowly: the apex domain `agiworkforce.com` is confirmed live and public, but **which commit it actually serves is stated as "Unknown"** — the apex is attached to a different Vercel account scope than the team-scoped project this audit could inspect, and the inspectable project's own production alias currently serves `4bfc99dc1` (~4 days behind `e15df56e3` HEAD, with 5 confirmed-missing routes and 5 of the last 20 deployments in `ERROR`).

The consequence is broader than the one paragraph it is confined to: **essentially every `COMPLETE`/`AHEAD`/`BROKEN` verdict in all twelve deliverables describes what commit `e15df56e3` on `compliance/dpdp` contains, not what any specific real user hitting `agiworkforce.com` today experiences.** That is a defensible and clearly-stated scoping choice for a source-code parity audit, and the DPDP-specific instance of it is called out well (`CurrentProductInventory.md:60-66`: the whole `compliance/dpdp` privacy surface "has never shipped to whatever build `agiworkforce.com` is currently serving"). But the generalization — that this is a **branch-and-commit audit**, not a live-product audit, and that gap is open for the entire feature surface, not only the DPDP pages — is never stated as a standing caveat at the top of any of the twelve deliverables. A reader treating this audit's `COMPLETE` labels as "verified live at agiworkforce.com today" would be relying on an assumption the audit itself never confirms and, per §1, cannot currently confirm at all.

**Next investigation:** this is explicitly flagged as `GAP-P0-003`, "blocked on human" (`PriorityExecutionPlan.md:645`) — someone with Vercel production access needs to identify which project/commit the apex domain actually serves. Until that is resolved, every deliverable would benefit from one added sentence at the top: _"Verdicts in this document describe the source tree at `e15df56e3`; the commit actually served at `agiworkforce.com` is unconfirmed (`GAP-P0-003`)."_

## 4. Contradictions and stale claims between deliverables

### 4.1 `voice_inject_text` — a stale, overstated security claim in a headline deliverable

This is the most concrete finding of this review: a claim in `CurrentProductInventory.md` — one of the twelve headline deliverables — is **directly contradicted by a later, more rigorous verification pass in this same audit round**, and the correction never propagated back.

**`CurrentProductInventory.md:251`** (§4.3, Desktop-Tauri):

> "`voice_inject_text` global dictation | **BROKEN** — its own Rust doc comment says 'must not be wired into an automatic dictation flow' pending an unlanded safety stage, but it _is_ wired into the live push-to-talk/global-dictation path; text can be injected into whatever field has OS focus, including password fields | `desktop-tauri.md:394-414`"

That sentence is sourced from the original `inventory/desktop-tauri.md:394-414` finding. But `gaps/domain-voice-media.md` (lines 156-174), filed later in the same audit round, explicitly corrects it:

> "**A correction to this domain's own brief and to a secondary inventory source** — The task brief for this domain, and `audit/parity-2026-08-15/inventory/desktop-tauri.md` §6, both assert that `voice_inject_text`'s documented safety precondition ... has been violated in shipping code. **Direct code verification shows this is false** ... `system_dictation_available()` is a hardcoded `false` ... so every `Global`-source dictation session is refused at admission ... Separately, the JS `injectText`/`voiceInjectText` action chain ... has **zero callers anywhere in the app outside its own definition** — it is not wired into the global-PTT flow or any other flow ... **This is not a live gap.**"

`GapMatrix.md`'s own final entry, `VOICE-MEDIA-012`, reflects the corrected picture and assigns it **P3** ("hardening/removed while unused," `security-gap`), explicitly noting it "is not a live bug today." `BackendGapAudit.md` §9.8 also carries the full correction ("Mitigating context, independently re-verified this round... not a live, exploitable bug in the current build"). So **two of the twelve headline deliverables (`BackendGapAudit.md`, and `GapMatrix.md` via `VOICE-MEDIA-012`) have the corrected, accurate, P3 picture. One headline deliverable (`CurrentProductInventory.md`) still asserts the original, now-refuted claim at full severity ("BROKEN," "text can be injected into whatever field has OS focus, including password fields") with no mitigating context and no pointer to the correction.**

This matters because `CurrentProductInventory.md` is the most likely single document to be skimmed for "what's broken" and reads, as currently written, like an active P0/P1-grade security defect (arbitrary text injection into password fields) rather than the correctly-assessed P3 finding it actually is (a dead, unreachable command with an unenforced-but-currently-moot precondition).

**Action needed:** update `CurrentProductInventory.md:251` to match the corrected finding in `gaps/domain-voice-media.md`/`VOICE-MEDIA-012` — reclassify from `BROKEN` to something like `DEAD (with an unenforced precondition, P3 hardening recommended)`, and cite `VOICE-MEDIA-012` rather than only the superseded `desktop-tauri.md:394-414`.

### 4.2 "Chat is genuinely shared" — a headline framing that is true at one altitude and misleading at another

The task brief named this exact pattern as worth checking, and it holds up under verification in the currently-published deliverables, not only in retired drafts.

`inventory/shared-packages.md`'s own headline (§0) is unambiguous: **"NO. There are at least four parallel chat implementations, and the shared package is not what the primary web surface actually renders."** `SurfaceParityMatrix.md:22` states the same thing just as plainly: **"Web's primary chat route does not use it."**

But two other places in the currently-published deliverables state a version of "chat is shared, not duplicated" without that qualifier, in a way that — read in isolation, which is how a synthesis-table row is usually read — implies more sharing than exists:

- **`CrossPlatformArchitectureAudit.md:196`** (§8.2): "**Chat is genuinely shared between Tauri-desktop and web, not duplicated** — both `apps/web/package.json` and `apps/desktop/package.json` depend on `@agiworkforce/unified-chat`..." This sentence is defensible as "a dependency exists in both manifests," but "between Tauri-desktop and web" reads as a claim about the product's two flagship chat surfaces sharing an implementation — which is false for web's primary route (`/`, `/chat`, `/chat/[sessionId]`, the surface most users hit) and true only for web's secondary Work/Code mode. The correct, qualified version of this claim appears nine lines above it in the very same document (§1, "Web (primary)... No — 3 named imports only") — the contradiction is resolvable by reading the whole document, but the §8.2 sentence does not carry its own qualifier and would mislead a reader who jumps straight to the Tauri/Electron section.
- **`CurrentProductInventory.md:279`** (§5, Desktop-Electron) and **`inventory/desktop-electron.md:324-332`** (its cited source) both state: "Shared renderer chat implementation | **COMPLETE / genuinely shared** via `@agiworkforce/unified-chat` — desktop's own `features/chat/` is 8 thin overlay files, not a parallel implementation." This one is more defensible in its narrow context (Electron's _own_ renderer, in bundled mode, loads the Tauri renderer, which really is the unconditional, flagship consumer of the shared package) — but presented as a bare "Shared vs. duplicated with `apps/web`" section header with no reference to the fact that this product has four total chat implementations and web's own primary one is not among the sharing surfaces, it produces the same risk: a reader of `CurrentProductInventory.md`'s Electron section alone would come away thinking chat-UI sharing between Electron/Desktop and Web is settled and complete, when the audit's own headline finding elsewhere is the opposite.

By contrast, `SurfaceParityMatrix.md` avoids this trap throughout — every place it touches the topic (lines 22, 34, 44) states the primary-vs-secondary distinction explicitly, even in single-line table cells ("PARTIAL — primary route bypasses it; secondary route uses it"). That document is the model to match.
