# SOC 2 and SOC 1 readiness assessment

Status: Current
Owner: Founder
Last updated: 2026-10-18

Whether AGI Workforce (AGI Automation LLC) can engage a CPA firm today for a
SOC 2 Type 1 report, a SOC 2 Type 2 report, or a SOC 1 report, measured
criterion by criterion against the 2017 Trust Services Criteria. Written for the
founder deciding when to engage, and for the auditor who will later ask what
existed on which date. Delete this file when a Type 2 report has been issued and
its exceptions are closed; until then, update a row when its gap closes rather
than adding a second document.

## Verdict

| Report          | Verdict                             | Why, in one line                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| SOC 1 (SSAE 18) | **Not applicable**                  | No user entity relies on AGI's controls for its financial reporting. Revisit only on the trigger in [SOC 1](#soc-1-applicability).                                                                                                  |
| SOC 2 Type 1    | **NOT READY, ready after 14 items** | No written policy set, no risk assessment, production alerting reaches nobody, and changes reach production with no enforced gate. 31 criteria have a design gap; the 14 steps in the path below close them in about 6 to 10 weeks. |
| SOC 2 Type 2    | **NOT READY**                       | Needs Type 1 readiness first, then at least a 3-month observation window of operating evidence, and security logs are deleted after 90 days today.                                                                                  |

Earliest realistic dates, if the founder starts the founder-only items in the
week of 2026-10-19 and nothing else slips:

- **SOC 2 Type 1:** controls in place as of **2027-01-15**; report in hand
  around **late February to March 2027**.
- **SOC 2 Type 2:** observation window **2027-01-15 to 2027-04-15** (3 months,
  the practical floor most firms accept for a first report) or to
  **2027-07-15** (6 months, the common first window); report about 4 to 8 weeks
  after the window closes.
- **Alternative rejected as the default:** skipping Type 1 and opening a Type 2
  window directly. It saves one audit fee but leaves nothing to show a buyer
  until mid-2027, and a design defect found at fieldwork becomes an exception in
  the only report. Choose it only if no deal needs a report before mid-2027.

The repository is unusually strong on technical controls (235 guards in CI,
append-only audit tables, hash-chained support access, an enumerated erasure
graph, checksummed migrations, weekly restore drills in CI). What is missing is
almost entirely the management layer a SOC 2 examines: policies, risk
assessment, vendor review, access review, and evidence that the production
configuration actually matches the code.

**Include Security (CC1 to CC9, mandatory) and Confidentiality (C1.1, C1.2) in
the first report.** Exclude Availability, Processing Integrity and Privacy.

## SOC 1 applicability

**Conclusion: a SOC 1 does not make sense now.** A SOC 1 reports on controls at
a service organization that are relevant to a user entity's internal control
over financial reporting (ICFR), and its users are the customer's management and
its financial statement auditors. The typical subjects are payroll processors,
claims processors, payment processors and loan servicers, systems whose output
lands in the customer's books.

AGI is an AI assistant and agent workspace. Reasoning from what it does:

1. **It is not a system of record for anyone's financial statements.** It does
   not process, calculate or store a customer's transactions, balances or
   journal entries.
2. **Its own billing is AGI's revenue, not the customer's ICFR.** Stripe
   handles subscription charges; a customer books an AGI invoice like any other
   vendor invoice, from the Stripe receipt.
3. **The finance connectors do not turn it into one.** The catalog defines
   QuickBooks, Xero, PayPal, Square, Plaid and a first-party bank-accounts
   connector as high-impact (`apps/web/lib/connectors/catalog.ts:237`).
   Production reports the bank-accounts connector disabled
   (`bank_accounts: disabled (PLAID_CLIENT_ID, PLAID_SECRET, PLAID_ENV not set)`,
   runtime log 2026-10-18 22:38Z). Whether the QuickBooks and Xero MCP
   connectors are connectable in production is **not verified**. Even where
   they work, the agent acts at the user's direction on a system the customer
   already controls; the customer's ICFR relies on QuickBooks or Xero, not on
   AGI.

**Trigger to revisit:** a customer's financial auditor names AGI as a relevant
service organization, or AGI starts posting entries or initiating payments in a
customer's ledger unattended, as a service the customer relies on rather than a
tool the customer drives. Then a narrow SOC 1 over those agent actions would be
the right instrument. A SOC 3 (the general-use summary of a SOC 2) costs little
extra once a SOC 2 exists and is the version to publish on `/trust`.

## Gap matrix

Legend for **Blocks**: **T1** is a design or implementation gap, so it blocks a
Type 1 (the control must exist and be in place on the report date) and therefore
also a Type 2. **T2** means the control is designed but has no operating
history, so it blocks only a Type 2. **Who**: F is the founder personally (an
account, a signature, a purchase, a decision); C is code or documentation an
agent can produce; F+C is both.

Criterion titles are paraphrased from the 2017 Trust Services Criteria; points
of focus are guidance, not requirements.
