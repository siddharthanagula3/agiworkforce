## Confirmed founder constraints and current demand experiment

On October 12 the founder confirmed no paying customers, explained that paid access is intentionally waitlisted to learn demand, and set a $0 monthly advertising budget. Preserve that gate. Absence of payments while checkout is unavailable does not measure willingness to pay.

Use existing tools for organic discovery; no ad spend, new paid acquisition tools or paid recruitment incentives are included. Founder labor and existing product-serving costs still count. Begin with one segment and one attainable task; the consultant recommendation remains provisional.

| Qualify interest | Explain the relevant published plan price, limits and intentional waitlist; invite a separate opt-in only after the prospect understands them | Price shown, intended paid job, permitted follow-up, next-task date and actual opt-in; no charge or access guarantee        |

A provisional decision to continue is three of the five eligible trial participants voluntarily returning for a distinct real task and taking the price-qualified waitlist step. Freeze this rule before starting and report all recruited/pilot and opportunity-eligible denominators. This small-sample rule directs founder effort; it establishes neither purchases nor PMF. A job that needs unavailable paid features cannot pass on a smaller Free task: record which claim was tested and leave the full workflow unresolved.

Keep recruitment consent distinct from product updates, analytics consent and the existing upgrade waitlist. Record the qualification fields in a permissioned research record; do not add private customer information to public repository documents. No form, database or consent flow was changed.

The outcome of this phase is a demand-qualified shortlist and observed task preference. If those hold, the founder can later decide whether to open a small paid pilot to test actual willingness to pay. Neither that future access change nor a nonzero advertising budget is authorized by this plan.

## Starting decision

**Test solo B2B research/strategy consultants who produce recurring vendor or competitor comparison briefs.** Start with one decision, approved public source material, a reviewed recommendation and an editable deliverable. An owner-operated small agency is a possible adjacent audience; do not mix its results into the solo-consultant sample initially.

The problem hypothesis is repeated human work between sources and an accepted client deliverable: assembling context, checking claims, applying new information, revising a recommendation and repairing the output. The job is served already. AGI needs a measured switching benefit against the participant's actual incumbent, including a manual worksheet if that is their normal method.

**Offer to test:** an AGI Web project for one recurring comparison brief, with reusable instructions and references, a draft to inspect, and an attainable output. Use the customer's actual requirements. Do not sell unattended client delivery or require a new automation platform.

**Primary outcome:** active human time to an accepted updated brief, with supported material claims and a usable editable export. Measure first-task setup and later reuse separately. A faster answer, attractive demo, signup or model catalogue is not this outcome.

The founder reported **no established users yet** on October 11. October 12 read-only inspection found recorded account/chat activity, but did not establish external customer qualification or retained demand. The sole active nonfree subscription row matched a Stripe test-mode resource; historical live cash receipts were not audited. The founder now reports no paying customers and confirms the intentional gate. Empty consent-gated analytics events do not mean zero usage. Exact counts, queries, corrected current Web persistence owners and limitations are in the [evidence record](../../docs/research/evidence/agiworkforce-pre-advertising-2026-10-12.json). Start with prospects and recent real tasks; independently classify existing account candidates before assuming this proposed segment is the best one. If actual retained external usage points elsewhere, update the recommendation from that evidence.

## Purpose and communication draft

The founder's current broader purpose is control over model/provider choice and where work runs. [AGI about, checked October 11](https://agiworkforce.com/about)

**Proposed why:** people doing the work should retain control over their context and tools, and devote more attention to judgment than repeated assembly of drafts.

**Positioning hypothesis:** for independent consultants producing recurring comparison briefs, AGI is a workspace for keeping approved references, decision criteria and reviewable drafts together. The benefit to prove is less checking, copying and rewriting through the next accepted deliverable.

**Headline:** “Turn a client brief into a draft you can review.”

**Support:** “Bring approved references and your decision criteria. Compare options, inspect the draft and revise it in AGI Web.”

**CTA:** “Try it with a public brief.”

**Invitation for discovery:** “I’m learning how independent consultants produce vendor comparisons. Could we spend 20 minutes walking through your most recent brief and tools? Public or redacted examples are enough. No purchase is required. If useful, we can discuss a separate workflow comparison.”

These are authored drafts, not customer language, measured promises or published copy. Keep the existing brand. Explain available model choice as support for the job; make surface, processing and plan limits explicit. The ecosystem mission can remain broad while this first invitation stays specific.

## Improve by reusing the current product

The smallest path is existing Web project instructions/files → chat draft or entitled research report → review/revision → existing artifact/export. Generic research starters already exist in [onboarding](../../apps/web/features/onboarding/lib/use-cases.ts); [ResearchPlan](../../apps/web/features/chat/components/research/ResearchPlan.tsx) and [ResearchReportView](../../apps/web/features/chat/components/research/ResearchReportView.tsx) own relevant plan, report and export behaviors. These are inspected implementation paths, not public quality proof.

Run the manual comparison before proposing a feature. If onboarding is the demonstrated bottleneck, consider an optional client-brief starter collecting audience, decision criteria, references and required format. If evidence review or export dominates, improve that existing owner instead. Keep any later change inside the repository's shared/native contracts and current release order.

Citation-link telemetry measures link/reference resolution, not whether the cited passage supports a claim. Saved-report follow-ups have bounded grounding. The pilot must verify claim support and preservation of requirements after updates; do not treat instrumentation, citations appearing or source-code presence as a quality pass.

## Screen ten prospects

Include solo B2B consultants who buy their own tools, already use an assistant, and completed at least three comparable vendor/competitor briefs in the last month. Ask for dates and an approved redacted example. Do not require dissatisfaction. Include people happy with their incumbent and record founder relationships/recruitment channels.

For a pilot, require a real upcoming brief, approved public/redacted sources, permission to process them in both workflows and a specified usable output. Log exclusions for access, confidentiality, job frequency and unavailable formats separately. Excluded prospects stay in the recruitment denominator; processing barriers are not automatically lack of demand.

Screen fields: participant ID, role, buyer, brief dates, current method/plan, output format, source restrictions, next task date, processing route and eligibility/exclusion reason. No credentials belong in these records.

## Twenty-minute discovery script

Ask permission for notes and explicit consent before recording. Disclose founder affiliation. Complete discovery before pitching or showing the product.

| Minutes | Questions                                                                                                                    |
| ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0–3     | Tell me about the most recent comparison brief: who requested it, when, and what decision did it support?                    |
| 3–8     | Walk through the actual steps from request to delivery. Which tools did you open? Can you show approved input and output?    |
| 8–12    | How did you decide it was ready? Show a checked claim, correction or revision. What supports your time estimate?             |
| 12–15   | What went smoothly? What slowed this brief? What happened as a result? When did that last happen before this?                |
| 15–18   | What have you already tried changing? What did you keep or stop using? Describe the last tool purchase or cancellation.      |
| 18–20   | When is the next comparable brief? Could an approved version be compared in two workflows? What would make that impractical? |

Follow with “Can you show an example?” or “What happened then?” Tag observations as artifact evidence, recollection or analyst interpretation. Preserve contradictory accounts. Do not ask whether a faster/better assistant sounds useful or turn hypothetical interest into willingness to pay.

## Five paired real tasks

Use five distinct real briefs from five practitioners. A bounded example is three vendors and four criteria, but retain the participant's genuine requirements; record any scope reduction. Freeze the same source packet, instructions, deadline, update, revision and acceptance checklist for both arms.

Each arm must extract requirements and facts, compare options, draft a recommendation, incorporate a genuine source update, handle the same client revision and deliver the agreed editable file. Include a real contradiction when one exists; do not manufacture one to claim a customer pain. Supplied-source drafting and open research are different tests.

Randomize execution order across participants; record learning from repeating the same task. Keep familiar incumbent context available. Count AGI onboarding/migration separately and in first-task total. Use fresh comparable task state and equivalent help policies. Conceal brands and randomize output review order where feasible; record when this is impossible.

Choose a proposed 60-minute active-time cap per arm before execution. Time setup, prompting, checking, correction, recovery and export until acceptance or the cap. Record elapsed waiting and cost/usage when accessible; missing cost remains unknown. Keep a founder-help ledger of minutes, action and trigger. Factual repairs, prompt rewriting and troubleshooting count as help; assisted work cannot pass the unassisted gate.

Retain first drafts, final files and source snapshots. The participant checks requirements; a second reviewer checks every material claim against the relevant dated passage. A material claim can change ranking, cost, eligibility or recommendation. A supported final draft does not erase defects in the first draft; record both.

**Acceptance:** required coverage and revisions complete; no final unsupported material claims or invented references; actual export opens and is editable, with usable tables/citations and no remaining repair. Record all repair work. If the required format is unavailable, mark the arm blocked rather than relaxing the acceptance rule after seeing it fail.

## Thirty-day sequence and decision gates

All actions below are proposed founder work, not scheduled or performed. Lock gates before recruitment; preserve original results if a gate later changes.

| Period     | Action                                                                | Output                                                                  |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Days 1–3   | Verify access and finish one attainable end-to-end public-source task | Verified flow/offer, frozen task rubric and shortlist                   |
| Days 4–7   | Conduct ten screened recent-task conversations                        | Artifact-backed job/pain observations and five eligible tasks           |
| Days 8–14  | Run the five paired comparisons                                       | First-use results, assistance ledger, accepted files and bottlenecks    |
| Days 15–28 | Observe two weeks of naturally occurring repeat work                  | Actual opportunities, chosen workflows, unassisted outputs and barriers |
| Days 29–30 | Review gates and choose continue, revise or stop                      | Founder decision and one justified next product/channel change          |

If paid access or the needed flow fails, resolve that specific blocker and restart timing; do not compress the repeat observation to preserve a calendar. No rollout, growth-rate or acquisition forecast is justified before a repeatable job is observed.

Proposed resource-allocation gates:

- **Discovery:** at least 6/10 show recurring work and a concrete setup, checking or output burden; at least three provide an approved upcoming task. Fewer matches means revise the job/segment, not polish a broader pitch.
- **Comparison:** at least 3/5 achieve 20% lower active time including first-task onboarding, no greater review time, accepted output and no founder rescue. Preselect this threshold with participants before results; report all five, blocked arms and first-draft defects. A severe quality failure overrides a speed gain.
- **Repeat:** at least 3/5 voluntarily initiate and complete a second distinct real brief unassisted and choose to continue at the reachable offer. No recurring prompts or rescue. Report all-five and opportunity-eligible denominators; insufficient repeat opportunities leaves this gate unresolved.

These tiny-sample gates guide founder effort. They do not prove market size, conversion, savings for all customers or product-market fit. An actual paid transaction is distinct from agreeing to try, a waitlist signup or hypothetical willingness.

## Commercial decision and open evidence

Do not reprice the product or promise cheaper equivalent subscriptions from this analysis. Use the canonical accessible offer; first learn what the participant already buys and whether AGI replaces, complements or adds to it. Measure provider/product costs plus human review work per accepted output. CAC, retention, margins and lifetime value are unknown, so no revenue or paid-acquisition forecast is supplied.

Continue only if the job recurs, the comparative benefit survives checking and formatting, and users independently return. If incumbent satisfaction remains high with no costly residual work, stop this segment experiment. If one real stage creates the friction, reuse its implementation owner for one small improvement and retest fresh tasks. Broader enterprise/developer expansion follows actual evidence and public availability.

The founder's first-customer stage is supported by the available evidence; observed pain frequency, comparative quality/time, output fidelity, real purchase decisions and actual acquisition performance remain open. This file owns the proposed experiment and measurement protocol; it does not duplicate defect registers or declare product capabilities complete.

**Proposed broad-spend gate:** extend to at least ten external paying accounts in the same segment, including people outside the founder's close network; observe two billing cycles and opportunity-adjusted repeat work; confirm contribution/payback and a reproducible source of qualified buyers. Set the loss cap and observation window before running, allow for delayed conversions, and stop immediately for a broken offer, unsupported claim, material quality/security failure or breached cap.

Use contribution after variable serving, payment, refund and support costs for payback. Report cash CAC and acquisition-labor-inclusive CAC separately. At zero verified external payers, CAC is undefined. The hypothetical economics table in the research is arithmetic for planning, not a recommended bid or measured margin.
