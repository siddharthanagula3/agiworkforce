# Domain: Legal, policy, trust & data-control surfaces — 2026-08-15

Benchmarked against live-observed ChatGPT, Claude, and Manus legal/policy documents (22 claims). Gemini contributed no claims to this domain's source set.

## Method note

The prior parity audit at `audit/parity-2026-08-15/` has **no legal/policy domain at all** — `gaps/domain-*.json` covers agentic-work, artifacts, backend-runtime, composer, cross-surface, dead-code, design-system, extensibility, memory, models, projects-files, rendering, search-research, settings, shell-nav-ia, voice-media. A grep of `GapMatrix.md`, `CapabilityMatrix.md`, and `CurrentProductInventory.md` for arbitration/liability-cap/DPA/AUP/training-opt-out/CBRN/biometric/CSAM returned zero hits. **Every finding in this domain is `NEW`** relative to the prior audit — there is nothing to confirm, contradict, or supersede.

One correction to the task brief: `legal-06` through `legal-09`'s `howToVerify` pointed at `apps/web/lib/server/managed-usage-policy.ts` as the place to check prohibited-use content. That file is actually the **managed-cloud credit/billing ledger module** (rolling caps, headroom, `sandboxTtlMs` consumption) — it has nothing to do with the Acceptable Use Policy. The real AUP content lives at `apps/web/app/acceptable-use/page.tsx` (351 lines), which is what this audit actually checked.

## What is genuinely strong here (read this first)

This codebase's legal surface is unusually mature for a product still in "public alpha" on its core paid offering. Findings below are gaps _relative to a very high bar already cleared_, not a description of a thin or neglected legal surface.

- **`apps/web/lib/legal-constants.ts`** is a real single-source-of-truth: entity name, notice address, governing law, venue, arbitration forum, contact routing, and every document's revision date are centralized and imported everywhere (verified: `terms`, `privacy`, `dpa`, `acceptable-use`, `mobile/legal` all import `LEGAL_ENTITY` from this file rather than hardcoding it). The file's own header documents that this fixed a real prior bug (two different notice addresses published on two pages). This is architecturally the exact fix for the anti-pattern `legal-22` describes at Manus (a stale affiliate claim surviving in one document after a sibling document updated) — grepped every hardcoded literal `"AGI Automation LLC"` outside the constants file and found none that conflict with the constant's current value.
- **`legal-01` (numeric liability cap):** `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:384-387` — "our aggregate liability... is limited to the fees you paid us in the 12 months preceding the claim, or 100 USD, whichever is greater." This is a near-exact structural match to ChatGPT's own 12-month/$100 pattern.
- **`legal-02` (disclosed dispute mechanism):** `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:409-440` — binding arbitration named with a real forum (`ARBITRATION_FORUM` = AAA Commercial Arbitration Rules, seated in Travis County TX per `legal-constants.ts:43-47`), class-action waiver, and a litigation fallback venue if arbitration is unavailable.
- **`legal-03` (dated arbitration opt-out window)** — this was flagged in the claim as a ChatGPT-only differentiator, and AGI Workforce already has it: `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:426-434` — "You may opt out of arbitration within 30 days of first accepting these terms" with a routed mailbox (`CONTACT_SUBJECTS.arbitrationOptOut`). This is a single-product ChatGPT capability in the benchmark that AGI Workforce has independently matched.
- **`legal-11` (DPA incorporated by reference):** confirmed end-to-end. `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:452` ("the DPA prevails on data protection matters"), and `/dpa` is linked from `apps/web/app/trust/page.tsx:481`, `apps/web/app/security/page.tsx:507-508,785`, and `apps/web/app/enterprise/page.tsx:146`. Not an orphaned document.
- **`legal-12` (in-product chrome links):** confirmed. `apps/web/features/settings/sections/HelpSection.tsx:70-75` has a "Legal" entry → `/legal` inside the authenticated Settings modal, and `PrivacySection.tsx:355-359` links directly to `/privacy`. This is exactly the gap the claim calls out Manus for — AGI Workforce does not have it.
- **`legal-13` (single indexed hub page):** `apps/web/app/legal/page.tsx` lists **19** documents in one table with per-document revision dates sourced from the same `POLICY_LAST_UPDATED` constant every document itself prints (so the index cannot claim a date a document doesn't have). This is a materially larger, more current legal footprint than the claim describes for Claude (~7 docs, no index) or Manus (4 docs, no index).
- **`legal-17` (third-party model reliance disclosed):** this is the standout finding. `apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx:373-379` and the Settings privacy panel (`PrivacySection.tsx:369-374`) both explicitly state AGI does not train its own models and that Managed Cloud prompts are sent to the selected provider (naming OpenRouter as the routed-model failover) under that provider's own terms. This is precisely the Manus-style "we are an orchestration layer over third-party models" disclosure the claim describes — and it is _more_ explicit than Manus's version because it also names the specific routing mechanism (OpenRouter failover) and links to `/subprocessors` for the live list.
- **`legal-05` / training-data toggle:** AGI Workforce does not train its own models at all, so the ChatGPT/Claude two-tier opt-out-consumer / opt-in-commercial pattern doesn't map onto this architecture — there is no training pipeline to gate. What's notable is _how_ this was handled: `PrivacySection.training.test.tsx` and a comment in `PrivacySection.tsx:390-394` record that an `improveModelTraining` toggle was **deliberately removed** because "nothing consumed it" — i.e. a previous version of this exact settings panel had a fake control that didn't gate anything, and it was cut rather than left to lie to users. That is the corrected form of exactly the "half-wired control" failure this repo's CLAUDE.md warns about. Correct classification: `DIFFERENT_BY_DESIGN`, not a gap.
- **`legal-08` (CSAM zero-tolerance + minors protections):** the AUP has an unconditional CSAM prohibition (`acceptable-use/page.tsx:230-236`) with no exceptions, and separately `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:125-134` has a real age-gating clause (18+ default, 13-17 only under a supervising parent/guardian/school account, under-13 barred entirely, with jurisdiction-specific higher ages named for the EU/UK/India). That's a substantive parallel to ChatGPT's separate "Keep minors safe" category, not just the bare CSAM line Claude has.
- **`legal-16` (no undisclosed ads):** confirmed no advertising business model anywhere in `privacy/page.tsx` (`398-...`: "We run no advertising, set no advertising cookies, and do not sell or share personal..."). Matches Claude/Manus's posture; correctly does not need ChatGPT's ad-terms stack.
- **`legal-07` (CBRN, no research exemption):** the AUP prohibits weapons development "including chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear, or high-yield explosives" (`acceptable-use/page.tsx:230-237`) with **no** research/educational carve-out anywhere in the document (grepped for "research purpose", "educational intent", "regardless of" — zero hits, meaning none exists to weaken the prohibition). Substance matches the benchmark; the one thing missing is that ChatGPT/Claude _explicitly say_ "regardless of stated intent" while AGI's version achieves the same effect by silence. Not filed as a gap — this is presentation, not substance.

## Gaps

### 1. `legal-06` — EU-AI-Act-style prohibited-practices list is present but thin, and confined to the wrong document (PARTIAL, P2)

The benchmark's 5-point list (biometric categorization for protected attributes, real-time public biometric ID, social scoring, workplace/education emotion inference, predictive policing from profiling alone) does **not** appear anywhere in `apps/web/app/acceptable-use/page.tsx` — the canonical, cross-surface AUP that governs web, desktop, mobile, both extensions, and the CLI. It DOES partially exist, but only in `apps/web/app/mobile/legal/page.tsx:255-264`, under an "EU AI Act disclosures" heading, and even there it names only 2 of the 5 items ("subliminal manipulation, biometric categorisation, or social scoring") — no mention of real-time public biometric ID, workplace/education emotion inference, or predictive policing from profiling alone.

So the actual state is: a real EU AI Act Art. 5 awareness exists in the codebase (someone clearly researched this for the mobile surface), but it (a) covers 2 of 5 items, and (b) is scoped to one surface's legal page rather than the canonical AUP that would give it force across the whole product. A majority of the benchmark (ChatGPT, Claude, Manus) independently converged on all 5 points in their primary usage policy.

- **ourEvidence:** `apps/web/app/acceptable-use/page.tsx` (full file read, section "(e) Content and conduct", lines 226-237) — no biometric/social-scoring/emotion/predictive-policing language. `apps/web/app/mobile/legal/page.tsx:261-263` — partial (2/5) version exists but is mobile-only.
- **Recommendation:** Port the mobile page's EU AI Act paragraph into the canonical `/acceptable-use` page's prohibited-uses section, and complete it to all 5 points (add real-time public biometric identification, workplace/education emotion inference, and predictive-policing-from-profiling-alone). Low-effort — the research and half the copy already exist in the repo; this is a content move plus 3 additional sentences, not new legal analysis.

### 2. `legal-09` — Automated high-stakes decision-making clause omits "medical" from its enumerated domain list (PARTIAL, P2)

`apps/web/app/acceptable-use/page.tsx:234-236`: "Do not use output to make automated decisions about a person — employment, credit, housing, insurance, education, or legal status — without meaningful human review." Six domains named, which is respectable (matches Claude's list length of 6), but the specific domain missing is **medical** — the one domain where an unreviewed automated decision carries the most severe possible harm, and the one every benchmarked product with this clause (ChatGPT, Claude) explicitly includes.

- **ourEvidence:** `apps/web/app/acceptable-use/page.tsx:234-236` (exact enumerated list, grepped for "medical" across the same file and `terms/page.tsx` — the only "medical" hit is an unrelated professional-advice disclaimer at `terms/page.tsx:238`, not this clause).
- **Recommendation:** Add "medical" to the enumerated list. One-word fix to an existing, otherwise-solid clause.

### 3. `legal-10` — No published commercial/enterprise legal-terms document distinct from consumer Terms (MISSING, P2)

All three benchmarked products maintain a business-tier legal document a prospect can read before ever contacting sales: Anthropic's Commercial Terms of Service, OpenAI's tier-sectioned Service Terms, Manus's MSA. AGI Workforce's consumer `/terms` applies by default to every tier; the enterprise path is a bespoke, unpublished negotiation: `apps/web/app/enterprise/page.tsx:109-112` — "MSA — We negotiate against your procurement. No forced click-through." `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:450-453` confirms the mechanism: "A master services agreement or order form signed by both parties prevails over these terms for the customer it covers... the DPA prevails on data protection matters." There is no file anywhere under `apps/web/app` matching a commercial/business/enterprise-terms pattern (searched for `*enterprise*terms*`, `*commercial*terms*`, `*msa*` route files — only the marketing page and its claims test exist).

This is a real design choice worth being explicit about rather than automatically "fixing": bespoke negotiation avoids publishing terms that could later be read as binding before a deal is actually signed, and the page is honest about that ("no forced click-through"). But it does mean a prospect cannot self-serve-review AGI's enterprise position the way they can with all three benchmarked competitors, which matters for a procurement-driven sales motion — the `/enterprise` page's own framing ("Pass the security review before you spend anything") argues for exactly the kind of pre-contract reviewability this gap withholds.

- **ourEvidence:** `apps/web/app/enterprise/page.tsx:109-112`, `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:450-453`. Absence confirmed by `find`/`grep` across `apps/web/app` for enterprise/commercial/MSA-named route files.
- **Recommendation:** Either (a) publish a standard-form commercial terms template (even one flagged "starting point, subject to negotiation") so procurement reviewers have something concrete to read pre-contact, matching the benchmark pattern, or (b) if bespoke-only is the deliberate choice, say so explicitly on `/enterprise` rather than leaving the absence to be discovered. This needs a founder/legal decision on which posture is intended — do not silently draft terms language without that sign-off, per `legal-constants.ts`'s own "FOUNDER CONFIRMATION REQUIRED" convention.

### 4. `legal-04` — Cannot observe a commercial-tier dispute-resolution stance because no commercial-tier document exists (MISSING, P3, depends on #3)

Directly downstream of gap 3: Anthropic deliberately reverses its own consumer stance (litigation-only) into mandatory arbitration for its higher-paying commercial customers. AGI Workforce has no commercial terms document, so there is no stance to observe — consumer `/terms` (arbitration + 30-day opt-out) applies uniformly by default until a signed MSA says otherwise. Not a defect today, but worth flagging so that if/when gap 3 is closed, the dispute-resolution choice for the commercial tier is made deliberately rather than drifting in by accident (i.e., don't silently inherit the consumer arbitration clause into a commercial template without an intentional decision).

- **ourEvidence:** Same as gap 3 — no commercial terms surface exists to check.
- **Recommendation:** Decide this at the same time as gap 3, not separately.

### 5. `legal-14` — No privacy-notice coverage for non-account-holder third parties named inside a user's conversation (MISSING, P3)

Claude publishes a separate "Non-User Privacy Policy" for people who are not Claude account holders but whose personal data may appear in the product (a third party named inside another user's chat, a person whose data surfaces via a connector). AGI Workforce's `/privacy` has no equivalent section — the closest related content is about _organizational_ data ownership (`privacy/page.tsx:702-712`: "abuse reports you filed about someone else keep the report and drop you"), which is about retention scoping, not about disclosing what happens to a non-user third party's personal data more broadly (e.g., a name and email pulled into a chat via a connected calendar or inbox).

- **ourEvidence:** `apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx` — grepped the full file for "non-user", "third part(y|ies)", "someone else", "another person" — only hit is the retention-scoping note at line 709, which does not address this. This is a genuinely single-product Claude differentiator (not table stakes), so severity is capped at P3.
- **Recommendation:** Given AGI's connector surface (Gmail, calendar, etc. pull third-party PII into a user's workspace), this is worth a short dedicated paragraph in `/privacy` even though it's not table stakes — the underlying scenario (connector pulls a colleague's contact info into a user's account) is real for this product today, more so than it likely was for the ChatGPT/Claude research pass.

### 6. `legal-18` — Sandbox retention window exists as a concrete number in code but is not disclosed with that number, and is not tier-differentiated in the policy (PARTIAL, P2)

Manus discloses an explicit numeric, tier-differentiated retention window for its cloud sandbox (7 days free / 14 days paid). AGI Workforce's `/privacy` retention table states only: "Sandboxes — Reclaimed once unreachable... A daily scheduled job reclaims abandoned sandboxes whose resume mapping has expired" (`apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx:628-634`). That is vaguer than what the code actually enforces: `apps/web/lib/e2b/reclaim.ts:31-36` defines `SANDBOX_MAX_AGE_MS = 24 * 60 * 60 * 1000` (24 hours) as "the longest a sandbox may live before it is reclaimed regardless of mapping state," with the comment explaining the underlying mechanism (a Redis `{tenant,user,conversation} -> sandboxId` mapping with a 24h TTL). That number is real, uniform across all plan tiers, and simply isn't surfaced to the user in the privacy policy.

Distinct from this: `packages/contracts/types/src/billing-catalog.ts:494-533` defines a per-tier `sandboxTtlMs` (10 min basic / 20 min pro / 30 min max / 60 min max_15x) — but that is a live-execution idle timeout, not a data-retention window, and is a different concept from Manus's multi-day persisted-state retention. So even if the 24h number were disclosed, it would not have Manus's tier-differentiation sub-claim, because the underlying mechanism genuinely isn't tier-differentiated for this dimension.

- **ourEvidence:** `apps/web/app/privacy/page.tsx:628-634` (the vague disclosure) vs `apps/web/lib/e2b/reclaim.ts:31-36` (`SANDBOX_MAX_AGE_MS`, the real 24h number) vs `packages/contracts/types/src/billing-catalog.ts:494-533` (the tier-differentiated but conceptually different `sandboxTtlMs`).
- **Recommendation:** Replace "Reclaimed once unreachable" with the actual number ("within 24 hours of the last mapping to your conversation"). This is a disclosure fix, not a capability build — the number already exists in code, it just isn't in the copy. If tier-differentiated retention is wanted to match Manus's exact pattern, that would require a product decision (today the code doesn't vary this by tier), which is a larger, separate piece of work.

### 7. `legal-21` — Consumer Terms/Privacy are a single worldwide document; no separate EEA/UK/Switzerland variant (MISSING, P3)

`GOVERNING_LAW` in `legal-constants.ts:40` is fixed to Texas with no regional branch, and `/terms`/`/privacy` are single documents applied globally — confirmed by reading both files in full; no conditional-by-region rendering exists. This is honestly disclosed rather than hidden: `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:585-591` states plainly, "We do not offer data residency in India or in the EU/UK, so using the service means your personal data is transferred there. If residency is a requirement for you, we do not meet it." The DPA (`apps/web/app/dpa/page.tsx:400-430`) does carry the substantive GDPR/UK-Addendum/Swiss-SCC transfer mechanics for enterprise customers, so the cross-border legal _mechanism_ exists — what's missing is only a full regional Terms-of-Use variant matching ChatGPT's "Rest of World" vs EEA/UK/Switzerland split.

- **ourEvidence:** `apps/web/lib/legal-constants.ts:40`, `apps/web/app/terms/page.tsx:585-591`, `apps/web/app/dpa/page.tsx:400-430`.
- **Recommendation:** Low priority — single-product ChatGPT differentiator, not table stakes, and the honest single-document disclosure is a defensible interim posture given AGI does not offer EU/UK data residency at all yet. Revisit only if/when regional data residency becomes a real product commitment; a regional ToS variant with no matching regional infrastructure would be worse than what exists today.

## Deliberate non-gaps (checked, and correctly not cloned)

- **`legal-19` (dedicated MCP marketplace listing policy):** AGI Workforce does not curate, vet, sign, or list third-party MCP servers at all — `apps/web/app/connectors/mcp-directory/page.tsx:120-125` states plainly "We do not mirror, curate, or sign any of them," pointing users to the official MCP registry and to AGI's own custom-connector dialog (bring-your-own endpoint). The AUP already disclaims vetting responsibility for custom MCP servers at `acceptable-use/page.tsx:214-224` ("AGI does not vet those servers... its operator sees the conversation context you send to its tools"). Since there is no marketplace, there is nothing to have a listing policy over — Claude's Directory Policy exists because Claude actually curates a directory. Building a marketplace just to then need a listing policy would be scope creep, not gap-closing. Correctly `DIFFERENT_BY_DESIGN`.
- **`legal-20` (positive-value-framed policy structure):** AGI's AUP uses Claude/Manus's flat-category style (lettered subsections under "Prohibited uses") rather than ChatGPT's four-value-statement wrapper. The claim's own `howToVerify` calls this "a presentation choice, not a substance gap" and says to treat divergence as non-blocking. Agreed — not filed as an actionable gap.
- **`legal-15` (feature-specific supplemental privacy notice):** Not applicable today. AGI Workforce has no sensitive-data integration comparable to ChatGPT's Apple Health integration (no health/biometric/financial-specific feature exists in the repo). Correctly nothing to build yet; worth remembering as a pattern if such a feature ships.
