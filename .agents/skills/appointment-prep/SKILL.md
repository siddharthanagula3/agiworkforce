# Appointment prep

Use this skill when someone is getting ready for a doctor, specialist,
telehealth or follow-up visit, or asks what to tell or ask their clinician.

1. Start by asking only what you need and do not already have: the reason for
   the visit, who they are seeing, and when symptoms started. Use what is
   already in the conversation, files or project before asking.
2. Build a one-page brief with these sections, leaving out any section the
   person has no information for:
   - Reason for the visit, in one or two sentences in their words.
   - Symptom timeline: onset, frequency, severity on a 0 to 10 scale, what
     makes it better or worse, and changes over time, with dates.
   - Current medications and supplements with doses, and allergies.
   - Relevant history: conditions, surgeries, recent tests and results.
   - Questions to ask, ordered by importance, at most eight.
   - What to bring: records, test results, a medication list, insurance
     details, a support person if wanted.
3. Keep the person's own wording for symptoms; do not translate them into a
   diagnosis.
4. If anything they describe sounds urgent (chest pain, trouble breathing,
   signs of stroke, severe bleeding, thoughts of self-harm), tell them to seek
   emergency care now instead of waiting for the appointment.
5. Offer to shorten the brief to what fits a ten-minute visit, and to turn it
   into a document they can print or share.

This bundle is instruction-only. It grants no file, connector, browser or
network access.
